# Benchmark and judgment policy

Use evidence in this order:

1. the account's preceding comparable period;
2. similar objects inside the same account;
3. the business model and lifecycle intent;
4. current official Klaviyo or mailbox-provider guidance;
5. third-party benchmarks, clearly labeled and dated.

## What makes a check good

A good check is observable, relevant, actionable, and denominator-aware. It has a clear source, period, confidence level, and consequence. It does not reward the mere presence of an object when activation, configuration, or performance evidence matters.

## Avoid false precision

- Do not hard-code universal open, click, conversion, revenue, or cadence targets.
- Do not fail a young, seasonal, B2B, or low-volume account against an ecommerce median without context.
- Treat machine-prefetched opens as directional. Prefer clicks, conversions, and attributed value for outcome comparisons.
- Current mailbox-provider complaint and authentication thresholds may change. Cite the official source and date if they determine a finding.
- A benchmark gap is a signal for investigation, not proof of causation.

## Recommendation language

Use “may,” “is associated with,” “test,” or “validate” for expected outcomes. Reserve causal language for an actual controlled experiment. Never promise revenue or inbox-placement gains.
